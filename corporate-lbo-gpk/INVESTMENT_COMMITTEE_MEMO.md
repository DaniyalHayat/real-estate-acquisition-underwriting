# Investment Committee Memorandum: Graphic Packaging Holding Company (NYSE: GPK)

**As of 16 October 2026 | Independent academic analysis | USD millions**

## Recommendation: Defer / reject at proposed terms

Under a hypothetical acquisition at **6.25x FY2025 unadjusted EBITDA**, funded with **4.25x debt / EBITDA** at entry, the model produces **18.7% gross five-year base-case sponsor IRR / 2.36x MOIC**. However, the stressed case results in **complete sponsor-equity loss**, rising debt and an illustrative leverage-covenant breach. Recommend **not approving** without lower-risk financing, a documented capex normalization plan, and quality-of-earnings work. This is not a real deal, lender commitment, or actual IC decision.

## 2. Transaction mechanics

- FY2025 calculated EBITDA: **$1,340m**.
- Hypothetical entry EV / EBITDA: **6.25x**, giving **$8,375m EV**.
- Hypothetical initial acquisition debt: **4.25x**, or **$5,695m**.
- Transaction fees: 2% of EV = **$167.5m**; debt-financing fees: 1% of issued debt = **$57m**.
- **Sponsor contributed equity = $2,904.5m**, using a simplified EV-plus-fees less new debt bridge.
- All pre-existing debt assumed refinanced within enterprise-to-equity value; management rollover, cash adjustments and purchase accounting are not modeled.

## 3. Forecast and capital structure

All 2026–2030 assumptions are analyst-designed scenarios, **not management guidance**.

**Base:** Revenue growth of 1%, 2%, 2.5%, 2.5%, 2.5%; EBITDA margin 16%, 16.5%, 17%, 17.25%, 17.5%; annual capex 7.5%, 6.5%, 6%, 5.5%, 5% of sales. Cash taxes 24%, D&A 6.2% of revenue, cost of new acquisition debt 8.5%, incremental NWC investment 1.2% of change in revenue.

**Upside:** 3–4% revenue growth before easing to 3%, EBITDA margin rising to 19.5%, 8% debt interest, exit multiple 7.0x.

**Downside:** -5%, -3%, 0%, +1%, +1% annual growth; EBITDA margin 14% to 15%; higher capex and 10.5% debt cost; 5.0x exit.

Annual operating cash available to pay down acquisition debt = EBITDA minus interest (charged on beginning debt), cash tax on positive EBT, capex and change in working capital. Positive cash swept to debt and negative cash increases debt. No interim distributions. It is a transparent simplified debt bridge, not a fully integrated consolidated three-statement acquisition model.

| Underwriting result | Downside | Base | Upside |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Five-year gross IRR | **-100%** | **18.7%** | **32.3%** |
| MOIC | 0.00x | 2.36x | 4.05x |
| Exit debt | 6,088 | 3,601 | 2,214 |
| Minimum headroom vs 5.5x test | **-0.16x** | +1.53x | +1.96x |

**Warning:** 5.5x is a *hypothetical* leverage restriction, not a verified existing GPK credit covenant or a lending term sheet. In downside, the sponsor's payoff is floored at zero under limited liability; creditor loss severity and restructuring are out of scope.

## 4. Investment returns attribution (base)

The **equity value gain** relative to invested sponsor equity is reconciled by:

- **Operating EBITDA growth contribution:** +$2,081m (change in EBITDA times 6.25x entry).
- **Deleveraging / debt reduction:** +$2,094m.
- **Exit multiple change:** $0 (6.25x exit equals 6.25x entry).
- **Fees paid:** -$224m.
- **Total equity gain:** +$3,951m, which reconciles to sponsor equity value at exit less sponsor equity contributed.

This is additive **dollar equity value**, *not* additive percentage-point IRR attribution. Base return does not depend on multiple expansion. It does depend on higher EBITDA and material debt repayments, which in turn depend on adequate capex normalization.

## 5. DCF valuation cross-check

A separate **unlevered DCF** values 2026–2030 forecast EBIT after tax plus D&A less capex and incremental working capital, discounting at **9.5% WACC**, assuming **2% perpetuity growth**, to an illustrative **$10,864m enterprise value** (~**8.11x** reported FY2025 unadjusted EBITDA). This output is highly sensitive to the terminal year, WACC and assumed capital-spending reductions. It is **not market evidence** of a 6.25x offer, and it is **year-end discounted**, not midyear discounted.

## 6. Investment diligence and rejection risks

1. **Operating quality:** Reconcile the FY2023–25 revenue and margin decline by business unit; segment performance, input costs and demand fluctuations.
2. **Capital intensity:** Separate sunk project spend, remaining growth capex, maintenance capex and packaging mill conversion spending. Validate ramp-up economics.
3. **Leverage:** Seek lender term sheets and test true covenant definitions, minimum liquidity, recovery waterfall, interest cover and refinancing maturities.
4. **Revenue / customers:** Evaluate demand mix, customer contract protection, price-volume spreads and substitution trends.
5. **Working capital:** Model balance-sheet schedules for receivables, inventory and payables; simple percent-change-revenue proxy insufficient for final underwriting.
6. **Value creation:** Develop an implementable, budgeted operating plan; verify actions for productivity, pricing and conversion investments.
7. **Market comps:** Obtain dated market quotes and documented M&A precedents. This version deliberately does **not** fabricate public-company multiple tables or deals.

## Decision gates

Do not advance without (i) historical EBITDA reconciliation and QoE, (ii) verified required capital investment, (iii) detailed working capital and true debt financing, (iv) a market-based entry price/premium assessment, and (v) robust stress-case liquidity. At current modeled terms **defer / reject**.

## Analyst limitations

This is an academic illustration using public records and explicitly hypothetical future assumptions; no buy-side firm, investment officer or committee approved it. The memo does not represent a real investment decision or a representation of live buy-side experience.
