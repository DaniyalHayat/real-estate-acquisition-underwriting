# Investment committee memorandum

**Park Hotels & Resorts | Historical acquisition case study**  
**Author: Daniyal Hayat | Financial base: FY2025 | Information cutoff: February 25, 2026**

## My recommendation

**Reprice or restructure before advancing.** At an assumed 10.5x entry and exit multiple, the base case generates a 12.2% five-year sponsor IRR and 1.78x MOIC, below my assumed 20% return hurdle. The upside case also misses that hurdle. The downside case requires $295.4m of additional equity and produces a capital loss. I would not support approval on these terms without stronger evidence of operating recovery, a lower purchase price, and a credible liquidity plan.

| Case | Five-year IRR | MOIC | Minimum DSCR | Peak debt / EBITDA | Additional equity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Base | 12.2% | 1.78x | 1.27x | 4.15x | $0.0m |
| Upside | 17.1% | 2.20x | 1.59x | 3.76x | $0.0m |
| Downside | -1.1% | 0.95x | 0.46x | 7.21x | $295.4m |


These are model outputs, not achieved investment returns. Entry EV is $6,394.5m; closing sponsor equity is $3,881.9m. The financing assumes 3.5x senior and 1.0x junior leverage against $609m reported adjusted EBITDA, at 7.5% and 10.5% interest respectively.

## Investment thesis and evidence

I chose a hotel REIT because property quality alone does not establish investment value: capital expenditure, debt service and cyclical demand determine how much cash reaches owners. Park's disclosed core portfolio gives me a way to compare property earnings with consolidated financing needs. The 20 core hotels generated $586m of hotel adjusted EBITDA on $2,006m of revenue in 2025. Hawaii, Florida and Puerto Rico introduce meaningful geographic and weather exposure. The core hotel list supports property-level screening; it does not prove insurability or replace catastrophe modeling. [Park supplemental data](https://www.pkhotelsandresorts.com/~/media/Files/P/Park-Hotels/reports-and-presentations/q4-25-supplemental-data.pdf)

Park's 2025 revenue was $2,541m, adjusted EBITDA $609m, operating cash flow $398m and capex $296m. The $277m reported net loss included substantial impairment expense, so I distinguish operating recovery from accounting charges. The gap between EBITDA and cash after capex is central to my debt-capacity assessment. [2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617406/000161740626000006/pk-20251231.htm), [earnings reconciliation](https://www.pkhotelsandresorts.com/~/media/Files/P/Park-Hotels/reports-and-presentations/q4-2025-earnings-press-release.pdf)

The thesis is that improving hotel demand and renovation productivity could expand margins and fund debt repayment. I encode that as a testable assumption, not a guaranteed catalyst. The base margin rises from 24.5% in 2026 to 27.0% in 2030, while capex declines from 11.0% to 7.0% of revenue. If assets need sustained elevated investment, that thesis weakens.

## Valuation and capital structure

The base DCF indicates approximately $5,917.4m enterprise value, below assumed entry EV. It uses a taxable corporate structure; retaining REIT status would need a different tax and distribution model. Trading comparisons with Host and Pebblebrook and two announced hotel acquisition precedents provide context, with explicit differences in capital structure and asset mix. I do not treat a median multiple as sufficient support for a purchase price.

Holding the base operating forecast, 4.5x initial debt and 10.5x exit multiple constant, the independent model reaches the assumed 20% hurdle near **8.67x entry EBITDA**. This is a sensitivity result, not a recommended executable bid; the model's purchase-basis simplification also changes depreciation with entry price.

## Credit and insurance downside

The base minimum DSCR is only 1.27x versus a 1.25x screening threshold. In the downside, recessionary revenue and margins, a 200bp rate increase and the illustrative $51m retained insurance cost lower minimum DSCR to 0.46x; peak gross leverage reaches 7.21x. This requires sponsor support even though the spreadsheet keeps cash above its minimum.

The insurance scenario links property damage, business interruption, general liability retentions and premium inflation to liquidity. Public filings establish that these exposures exist, but they do not provide the loss runs or detailed policy terms needed to price a commercial casualty account. Pebblebrook's public LaPlaya discussion offers a real example of operational disruption and insurance recoveries, without being used as Park loss experience. [Park 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617406/000161740626000006/pk-20251231.htm), [Pebblebrook results](https://investor.pebblebrookhotels.com/news-1/news-releases/news-details/2026/Pebblebrook-Hotel-Trust-Reports-2025-Results-and-Provides-2026-Outlook/default.aspx)

## Diligence conditions

1. Validate hotel-level operating budgets, renovation schedules, maintenance capex and the effect of planned dispositions.
2. Obtain lender terms, debt payoff and change-of-control provisions, collateral valuations and monthly liquidity forecasts.
3. Reconcile consolidated versus JV economics, minority interests and cash distributions before converting enterprise value into a binding equity bid.
4. Determine viable REIT / taxable corporate structure and quantify transfer taxes, interest limitations and purchase accounting.
5. Review five years of loss runs, property schedules, construction and protection details, policy wording, retentions, aggregates and insurer recoverability.
6. Reassess entry price against downside cash needs and negotiate financing that does not depend on uninterrupted recovery.

My decision would change with verified operating improvements, lower total purchase cost or financing that materially reduces downside funding needs. See [methodology](METHODOLOGY.md) for the full scope and accounting limitations.
